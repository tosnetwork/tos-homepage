Issuance policy

Bounded bootstrap. Validator-issued.

TOS has no fixed maximum supply. A bounded validator bootstrap creates approximately 10,000,000 TOS over roughly its first two years — the 101,000-TOS genesis bootstrap is included — after which governance targets a low perpetual issuance of about 2% of the prevailing supply per year. All native issuance comes only from finalized blocks produced by elected validators. Service work, provider claims, and off-chain scores cannot create TOS.

Two-year bootstrap target ~10M TOS

A calibration target, not a cap: genesis plus about two years of validator rewards.

Provisional genesis 101,000 TOS

100,000 bootstrap + two 500-TOS system reserves.

Post-genesis validator issuance ~9.899M TOS

Created only with finalized blocks over the ~2-year bootstrap; then about 2% of supply per year.

Insider allocation 0 TOS

No team, investor, foundation, ecosystem, or treasury allocation.

How validator rewards are created

Blocks fund the elected validator set.

Produced block → ConfigParam 14 → Elector bonus pool → Effective-stake share

No finalized block means no native creation. Outages create no reward debt, catch-up multiplier, or later backfill. Transaction and service fees transfer existing TOS and are a separate, continuing flow.

How genesis is constrained

Bootstrap wallets are temporary.

Four original validators start with equal consensus weight. Each control wallet may receive at most 20,000 TOS of stake principal plus up to 100 TOS of metered bootstrap costs. After two successful elected- set handovers, the main wallet's remaining balance must be burned.

How concentration is constrained

Open elections replace administrative allocation.

Equal bootstrap funding, stake limits, recurring elections, operator- control disclosure, and public concentration metrics reduce single- entity dominance. Four validators are only the bootstrap minimum; the public-participation target is 64 independent operators.

How issuance continues

Bootstrap gives way to ~2% issuance.

Genesis creation and every validator block reward count toward the ~10,000,000-TOS two-year bootstrap target — a calibration target, not a hard cap. After the bootstrap, governance re-parameterizes ConfigParam 14 so annual creation tracks about 2% of the prevailing supply, a low perpetual issuance that keeps validators securing the network.

Distribution controls reduce concentration risk; they cannot guarantee dispersed ownership.

Early rewards necessarily go to the validators then elected, and stake-proportional rewards may compound existing holdings. TOS provides no equity, debt, dividend, redemption, fixed yield, liquidity, or price support. Market value can fall, and participation remains subject to technical, custody, governance, concentration, and regulatory risk.
