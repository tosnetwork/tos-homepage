Token launch discipline

Product first. Emission last.

Native TOS secures validators, pays network fees, and supports escrow, bonds, and final settlement. Validator rewards follow produced blocks. AIPoW remains feature-gated off and creates nothing unless product, organic-demand, security, legal, and decentralization gates pass. The five-billion supply figure and 4.5-billion community-agent allocation are provisional policy targets, not launch entitlements or hard caps.

AIPoW at genesis OFF

No community-agent allocation is created before every activation gate passes.

How validator rewards are created

Blocks fund the elected validator set.

Produced block → ConfigParam 14 → Elector bonus pool → Effective-stake share

No finalized block means no native creation. Outages create no reward debt, catch-up multiplier, or later backfill. Transaction and service fees transfer existing TOS and continue after block creation is stopped.

How AIPoW is constrained

No product-market fit, no activation.

Activation requires repeat organic payers, non-subsidized provider retention, frozen receipt and scoring methods, independent scorers, public shadow epochs, attack testing, audits, governance review, and then-current legal analysis. A failed gate creates no emission debt.

How development is sustained

Zero insider allocation is not a funding plan.

Before production launch, the project must publish the legal and operating entities, material funding sources and uses, conflicts, maintenance runway, and the boundary between commercial products and protocol governance. Grants, service revenue, equity, and protocol issuance must remain separately disclosed.

How monetary policy evolves

Measured demand recalibrates policy.

Any AIPoW proposal must revisit its coefficient, epoch ceiling, cold-start floor, and cumulative cap using organic settled demand, provider margins, security needs, and concentration data. Preserving a headline allocation is never a reason to emit into circular demand.

Distribution controls reduce concentration risk; they cannot guarantee dispersed ownership.

Early rewards necessarily go to the validators then elected, and stake-proportional rewards may compound existing holdings. TOS provides no equity, debt, dividend, redemption, fixed yield, liquidity, or price support. Market value can fall, and participation remains subject to technical, custody, governance, concentration, and regulatory risk.
