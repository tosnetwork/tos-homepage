# TOS Homepage Content Authority

## Purpose

The TOS homepage is a public explanation and navigation surface. It is not a
constitutional record, normative specification, conformance certificate,
deployment record, finalized-state observer, security audit, legal opinion, or
production-readiness authority.

This file defines how homepage claims are derived and reviewed so that a clear
marketing sentence cannot silently replace a stronger source of truth.

## Authority order

| Question | Controlling evidence |
|---|---|
| What institutional direction and limits are proposed or adopted? | The exact Agentic Internet Constitution version and its ratification record. The current published Constitution is a founding proposal and is not yet ratified. |
| What do shared TOS service objects and actions mean? | The exact adopted `tos-service-spec` version, profile, schemas, invariants, and vectors. |
| What does a reference library, Gateway, Agent, or product do? | Its exact source revision, build, configuration, dependencies, and observed execution. |
| What happened on TOS? | Independently verified finalized TOS state in the exact network domain. |
| What happened through a direct or external settlement path? | The Agreement-selected evidence and authority for that path, never a TOS Gateway projection. |
| What is safe, deployed, adopted, lawful, or profitable? | The distinct review, deployment, demand, legal, and economic evidence required for that exact claim. |

The homepage must not invent a convenient common authority when these sources
answer different questions.

## Required status vocabulary

Homepage status claims must distinguish at least:

1. `PROPOSED` — architecture or policy without qualifying implementation;
2. `IMPLEMENTED` — exact source and tests exist;
3. `LOCALLY_VALIDATED` — bounded local or same-host execution evidence exists;
4. `INDEPENDENTLY_REPRODUCED` — a separate operator or implementation reproduced the claim;
5. `PUBLIC_TESTNET_ACCEPTED` — the exact current domain passed its adopted public-testnet gate;
6. `PRODUCTION_ACCEPTED` — deployment, custody, monitoring, recovery, and release gates passed;
7. `EXTERNAL_DEMAND_PROVEN` — arm's-length repeat use and economic evidence exist; and
8. `INCONCLUSIVE` or `BLOCKED` — required evidence is missing, ambiguous, stale, or failed.

Source presence, CI, a local test, a cinematic UI, a Gateway record, a signed
message, a transaction submission, finalized settlement, and external revenue
are different claims.

## Non-derogable content rules

- The Agentic Internet must not be presented as a TOS-owned product, chain,
  marketplace, or jurisdiction.
- TOS is a founding proposer and reference ecosystem, not universal commercial
  authority.
- An Intent is not an Agreement or payment authority.
- Direct trust, authenticated off-chain evidence, TOS escrow, and external
  settlement must remain distinguishable choices.
- A protocol specification, reference implementation, Gateway projection, and
  finalized chain state must not be described as interchangeable authority.
- A blockchain record may prove an adopted identity, control, claim, or
  commitment. It must not be presented as automatic legal ownership of
  physical, intellectual, or off-chain property.
- A reference implementation must not be presented as the only way to conform.
- Public visibility must not be described as open licensing. Protocol and code
  openness require explicit licenses and independent conformance evidence.
- OpenFox model output, learned Skills, MCP content, and catalog entries are
  proposals or inputs, not authority.
- A campaign result may inform a separate promotion decision but must not
  directly enlarge permissions, budgets, custody, network access, or production
  exposure.

## Review triggers

Re-review the homepage when any of the following changes materially:

- constitutional status, scope, or version;
- normative protocol identifier, schema, profile, or authority boundary;
- Root Gate O and Gate C through Gate G evidence;
- first commercial acceptance target;
- settlement, custody, asset, or finality rules;
- OpenFox autonomy, capability admission, promotion, or owner-control behavior;
- token policy or AIPoW activation requirements;
- repository or documentation location;
- license, trademark, media, privacy, or legal terms; or
- a public claim of deployment, adoption, revenue, interoperability, security,
  certification, or production readiness.
